Market Update — Executive Summary

The tape changed character this week. Price slipped, led by QQQ down about 3% to 728, while SPY eased 0.9% to 771.79. Yet inside the S&P 500, participation improved sharply: members above their 50-day jumped from 35.2% to 44.1%, and above their 200-day from 47.8% to 51.6%, back over the 50% line. Money is rotating out of mega-cap tech into the rest of the index, and Transports ($DJT) rallied +1.4% on the day to confirm it.

The broad market did not join in. Across roughly 4,800 US stocks, the share above the 200-day fell to 37.5%, the lowest reading of the past year. Small caps remain the weak leg. Meanwhile, crash insurance got even more expensive: SKEW rose to 160.45, a new three-month high, as VIX and VVIX also climbed.

Price vs Internals — The Read

  • Rotation, not liquidation: QQQ fell harder than SPY while more S&P members moved above their averages. Leadership is broadening away from mega-cap tech, which is healthier for equal-weight than for /NQ.
  • Two-speed market: large-cap 200-day breadth is back at 51.6%, but the full market sits at 37.5%, a one-year low. Small and mid caps are still being sold.
  • Cyclicals waking up: Transports up 1.4% on the day and the dollar ($DXY 101.98) softening support the rotation story.
  • No washout, no thrust yet: the 50/200 ratio at 0.71 is still below parity. A move above 1.5 would be the historically strong thrust signal; nothing near that yet.

SKEW climbing while index put/call fell means the protection being bought is concentrated in far out-of-the-money strikes: cheap-looking lottery tickets against a gap down rather than broad hedging. On its own SKEW is not a timing signal, but at extreme levels with weak broad breadth and rising VVIX, it argues for defined-risk positioning over naked short puts.

Broad Market Still Weak
