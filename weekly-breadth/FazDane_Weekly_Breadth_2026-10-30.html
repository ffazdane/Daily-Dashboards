Weekly Summary

The week started with three up days, then broke hard. Friday was the S&P 500's biggest one-day drop since June 5 (−1.82%), and the Nasdaq fell 2.86% as Amazon and Alphabet earnings revived fears about AI spending and cash flow, and Intel had its steepest one-day drop on record. For the week the S&P lost 0.58% and the Nasdaq 2.0%, both a third straight weekly decline.

Breadth tells a different story. Small caps gained 1.3% on the week, and the share of US stocks above their 50-day average climbed to 37.6%, its third straight weekly rise. In October, 9 of 11 S&P sectors rose even though the index fell about 1%. The cap-weighted indexes are being pulled down by AI and semiconductor leaders while the typical stock is recovering.

Breadth Read

  • Positive divergence is widening: 50-day breadth rose 11 points in three weeks while /NQ fell about 8.5%. Selling is concentrated in a handful of heavyweights: NVIDIA, Intel and AMD alone cost the S&P roughly 52 points on Friday.
  • Small caps lead: the Russell 2000 gained 1.3% on a week the Nasdaq lost 2%. Transports ($DJT) closed at 20,329, their best level of the month.
  • Long-term regime not repaired: only 39% of stocks hold their 200-day. Breadth is improving from deeply oversold levels, but it is still well below the 50% line that marks a healthy market.
  • Defensive tilt: October's winners were energy, healthcare, utilities and staples. That is a broadening, but a cautious one, not a risk-on rally.

What Drove the Week

  • AI spending and cash flow: Amazon's trailing free cash flow swung to a record outflow of more than $42 billion, and Alphabet had its worst day in more than 20 years. The Nasdaq-100 lost roughly $2 trillion in value in October.
  • Semiconductors: Intel fell about 29%, its steepest drop on record, on a cash-burn warning. The SOX fell 7.2% Friday and 15.3% for the month.
  • Fed: September PCE came in hot and officials Musalem and Barkin said rates may need to rise; markets price a December hike. The 10-year still fell about 36 bps in October to roughly 4.93%.
  • Oil and risk events: WTI near $103–104 and Brent near $117 on Iran strike risk; a $6.3B Binance hack sent Bitcoin to about $77,000; gold hit a 52-week low.
