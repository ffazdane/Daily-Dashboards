Weekly Summary

Last week's volatility inversion turned out to be a buying signal. The S&P 500 rallied 2.5% to 7,561.67 and reclaimed its 30-week average, the Nasdaq gained 2.3% and the Dow 2.1%. All 11 sectors finished higher, and about 79% of S&P 500 stocks rose on the week. VIX fell 25%, from 25.64 to 19.30, and the VIX/VIX3M curve returned to contango after a two-day inversion.

The quality of the rally faded as the week went on. Participation dropped from 66.6% of S&P stocks rising per day in the first half of the week to 51.1% in the second half. October CPI came in hot (headline +0.4%, core +0.3%, both above forecast), keeping a December rate hike in play, and tech fell 2% on Friday. The composite breadth reading for all US stocks actually slipped from the 51st to the 41st percentile. Prices bounced; the market's deeper internals have not yet confirmed it.

Breadth Read

  • Short-term breadth keeps improving: stocks above their 50-day rose for a fifth straight week to 41.4%, up 15 points since mid-October. Among S&P 500 members, 63% now trade above their 200-day, up from 55% a week ago.
  • The deeper gauges did not follow: the composite of 14 breadth indicators fell to the 41st percentile. The NYSE McClellan Summation Index sits at −170, 10-day new highs minus new lows is −2.8%, and the NYSE advance-decline line is well below its 52-week high.
  • Equal weight leads: the equal-weight S&P is 2.6% ahead of cap-weight over three months (84th percentile). The rotation away from mega-cap tech that began in October is intact.
  • Large caps healthier than small: composite breadth is at the 65th percentile for S&P 500 members but only the 43rd for Nasdaq-100 members and the 32nd for all Nasdaq-listed stocks.
  • Cyclicals recovered: Transports rallied 4.1% to 20,594, their best close in our six-week series.
  • Fading into Friday: participation thinned through the week, and Friday's tech drop (−2.0%) came as Nvidia headed for its longest weekly losing streak since 2012.

The capitulation signal flagged in the last three reports did its job: after past VIX/VIX3M inversions, SPY's median return was +0.75% over one week and +2.54% over one month, versus +0.40% and +1.56% for all days. This time the first week delivered more than the typical month. The caution now is the opposite of last week's: with SKEW low and realized volatility above VIX, the market is under-hedged relative to how much it is moving.

Summary & Actionable Conclusions

Inversion Paid

The Nov 5–6 VIX/VIX3M inversion marked the low; SPY gained about 2.6% the next week and every sector rose.

Breadth Improving

50-day breadth up five straight weeks to 41.4%; equal weight leading cap weight; Transports at a six-week high.

Not Yet Confirmed

Composite breadth fell to the 41st percentile, 200-day breadth is below 40%, and participation faded late in the week.

Cheap Options

Realized volatility (22.1%) is above VIX (18.7) and SKEW is the lowest in six weeks. Hedges are inexpensive now.

Levels to Watch

SPX support 7,500 / 7,350 / 7,240; resistance 7,650 / 7,700 / 7,840. Breadth: 50% on the 50-day, 40% on the 200-day.

Bias & Plan

Cautiously constructive. Hold gains in equal-weight and cyclicals, add on a confirmed break above 7,650, and use cheap volatility to hedge.
