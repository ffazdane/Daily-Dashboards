The pattern is remarkably consistent: a drawdown exposes a hidden weakness, public anger forces Washington to act within roughly one to three years, a new safeguard is built — and then, over the following decade or two of calm, the rules are loosened until the next break. Every major U.S. market safeguard investors rely on today — the SEC, FDIC insurance, SIPC, circuit breakers, PCAOB audits, bank stress tests — was born from a specific crash.

The speed depends on who can act. Exchanges and the SEC can change trading mechanics in weeks — single-stock circuit breakers arrived about a month after the 2010 Flash Crash. Structural reform that needs Congress (the 1930s Acts, Sarbanes-Oxley, Dodd-Frank) typically lands 2–5 years later, often after the market has already bottomed.

Built-in brakes: market-wide circuit breakers

Born from the 1987 crash (when the Dow lost 22.6% in a single day with no pause button) and recalibrated after the 2010 Flash Crash, today's S&P 500-based halts are:

Checks and balances — who watches whom

No single body controls market regulation. Each layer can push, block or reverse another — which is why rules both get built after crashes and get unwound in calm periods.

Congress

Legislates · funds · oversees
  • Writes the statutes (1933/34 Acts, SOX, Dodd-Frank, GENIUS)
  • Can repeal rules (EGRRCPA 2018) or veto via Congressional Review Act
  • Confirms regulators; holds hearings

Executive

President · Treasury · FSOC
  • Appoints SEC, CFTC, Fed, FDIC leaders
  • Treasury chairs FSOC & the President's Working Group (est. 1988)
  • Sets the deregulatory or enforcement tone

Independent regulators

SEC · CFTC · Fed · FDIC · OCC
  • Write and enforce rules within their statutes
  • Fed is lender of last resort; FDIC insures deposits
  • Bank capital & stress tests (post-2010)

Courts

Judicial review
  • Loper Bright (2024) ended Chevron deference to agencies
  • SEC v. Jarkesy (2024) — jury trials for SEC fraud penalties
  • 5th Circuit vacated SEC private-fund adviser rules (2024)

Self-regulators

FINRA · exchanges · PCAOB
  • FINRA (ex-NASD, 1938 Maloney Act) polices brokers
  • Exchanges run circuit breakers & LULD
  • PCAOB (SOX 2002) inspects auditors

Market & public

Investors · press · states
  • Prices punish bad policy fast (April 2025 tariff pause)
  • Whistleblowers, journalists, short sellers expose fraud
  • State AGs & regulators fill federal gaps

Where we are now — 2025 to 2026

  • April 2025 tariff shock: the S&P 500 fell 18.9% on a closing basis (21.4% intraday) from the Feb 19 high to the April 8 low. The "check" here was not a regulation but a policy reversal — the April 9 90-day tariff pause produced a 9.5% one-day rally, and the index recovered its losses within months.
  • 2026 drawdowns stayed shallow: the Iran-war sell-off from late February took the S&P to roughly 9% below its high in late March; State Street puts the index's maximum 2026 drawdown at 8.9%, even as global semiconductors fell nearly 30% from peak by Nov 6.
  • Bank capital loosened: the Fed, FDIC and OCC finalized a cut to the enhanced supplementary leverage ratio for the largest banks on Nov 25, 2025 (effective April 1, 2026), over dissents from Fed Governors Barr and Cook.
  • New framework for stablecoins: the GENIUS Act (signed July 18, 2025) created the first federal stablecoin regime — new rules, but designed to grow the industry.
  • SEC reset: Chair Atkins withdrew a slate of Gensler-era proposals, is pursuing a "minimum effective dose" disclosure regime, has proposed rescinding the climate-disclosure rule, and is weighing semiannual instead of quarterly reporting.

FazDane read: the pendulum is in a loosening phase similar to 1999–2006 and 2017–19. That does not predict a crash — those phases also coincided with strong markets — but it does mean the shock absorbers are thinner going into the next stress event, and history says the response will come after the damage, not before.

Summary & actionable conclusions

Crashes write the rules

Nearly every safeguard — SEC, FDIC, SIPC, circuit breakers, PCAOB, stress tests — was a direct reaction to a specific drawdown.

Rules erode in calm

Glass-Steagall lasted 66 years; Dodd-Frank's bank thresholds were raised in 8. SVB failed 5 years after that rollback.

Reaction lag ≈ 2 years

Trading-mechanic fixes take weeks; Congress takes years. Don't expect regulation to rescue a falling market.

Breakers buy time, not floors

7/13/20% halts and LULD slow panics but don't stop them — 2020 still fell 34% in 33 days.

2026: loosening phase

Bank leverage cut, SEC disclosure lighter, courts constraining agencies. Fewer brakes if stress arrives.

Trader takeaway

Recoveries since 1987 have been faster, partly thanks to these backstops — but size positions for drawdowns, not for the rescue.
